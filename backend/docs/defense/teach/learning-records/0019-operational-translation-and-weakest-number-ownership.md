# Operational translation + weakest-number ownership

Slide 17 introduces the two craft skills that govern how results tables are presented for the rest of the deck. **Operational translation**: a metrics table earns its slide only when every number is converted into a consumer behaviour — eight F1 decimals become four behavioural tiers (trust / spot-check / verify / correct) and five spoken instructions ("dates and locations — trust them … districts and victims — those need the most correction"). The instruction, not the decimal, is what the panel remembers and what the institutional consumers (AU-CEWS, ACLED) would act on. **Weakest-number ownership**: VICTIM 0.817 is the lowest number on the slide, and the candidate explains it unprompted with a causal account — an owned weakness is context, a discovered weakness is a finding. The two skills together are the evidence-section posture from slide 16 (honest-metric reframe) applied row by row: operational truth over flattering summary.

## Implications

- The operational-translation move has a reusable three-step shape: (1) band the numbers into behavioural tiers — nobody remembers eight decimals, everyone remembers four instructions; (2) phrase each tier as what the consumer *does*, not what the model *scores*; (3) connect to the workflow claim — the reliability map is what makes review-not-rewrite actually save time, because the analyst knows what to skim and what to read carefully. This generalises to slide 19 (UAT Likert table → what users could and couldn't do) and to any future results presentation.
- The VICTIM account is a three-parter with a judo turn: cause 1 (low support — 5,492 spans vs 47,612 for ACTOR, a 9× signal gap), cause 2 (extreme phrasing variability — "civilians" to "the bus driver's family"), context (0.817 *is the lifted number* — plain CE gives 0.708; the recipe bought back 11 points; the remainder is structural, with named paths in limitation 2 and future work 4). The judo turn answers the barbed form of the question ("isn't VICTIM what matters most?"): *because* it matters most, the entire training recipe targets it. The weakest number becomes the motivation for the contribution. This turn is the slide's highest-value rehearsal item.
- The support × phrasing-regularity two-by-two is the load-bearing causal model behind the whole table: DATE (common + formulaic → 0.956, strongest), CASUALTIES (rare + formulaic → 0.885, strong despite rarity), DISTRICT (common + ambiguous → 0.826, weak despite 21 k spans), VICTIM (rare + variable → 0.817, weakest). Support predicts F1 only with phrasing regularity held constant. Naming CASUALTIES as the mirror case is what proves the causal stories are real rather than post-hoc — it is the prepared answer to the smartest attack the table invites ("DISTRICT has 4× VICTIM's data but the same F1 — doesn't that break your explanation?").
- The precision-greater-than-recall uniformity (all eight rows, no exceptions) gets the "read the gap's direction out loud" treatment from slide 16's micro/macro move. Three-step reading: mechanism (uncertain → defaults to O), direction-is-right-for-this-consumer (a false alarm costs a deletion and erodes trust in every other chip; a miss costs one addition the analyst was scanning for anyway), tunable knob (per-category confidence thresholds, §4.7, can trade P for R per use case). "Calibrated to miss rather than to guess" is the reframe that converts the observation from flaw to design before the panel can frame it first.
- The significance answer has its own honesty deposit: concede the per-row variance on rare entities immediately, defend only the robust structure (14-point DATE-to-VICTIM spread, 3–5-point tier gaps, rankings stable across all three seeds), and *name a specific difference you do not defend* — REGION 0.891 vs CASUALTIES 0.885 is within seed noise (±0.4 pp) and the slide draws no conclusion from it. Policing your own table harder than the panel would, again.
- The reservation graph at this point: slide 17 may name the VICTIM endpoints (0.708 → 0.817) if directly forced, but the four-configuration progression (CE 0.708 → weighted 0.776 → focal 0.792 → combined 0.817), the deltas, and the significance analysis are slide 18's exclusively. The Goma/Beni CITY-DISTRICT confusion pattern is slide 20's (confusion matrix). The eight-row recital itself is the evidence section's second memorisation artefact, after slide 15's 10-step pipeline — same eyes-closed × 5 drill protocol, with the tier scaffold (2-2-2-2) and the descending-order property as built-in error checks.

## What this does not yet establish

- The eight-row recital has not been drilled to fluency. Unlike slide 15's pipeline (where the steps have narrative flow), the F1 ranking is pure paired-associate memorisation — entity name + three-digit decimal × 8. The tier scaffold helps (956/934 · 923/891 · 885/866 · 826/817) but the middle pairs (REGION/CASUALTIES and ACTION boundaries) are the predicted stumble sites because the tier boundary falls between 891 and 885 — only 6 points apart and easy to transpose. A transfer drill beyond the recital: name any entity out of order → give F1, tier, and one-line causal story within three seconds.
- The judo turn ("because it matters most, the recipe targets it") has not been tested under interruption. The risk: a panellist interrupts after "low support" with "so your system fails exactly where it's needed" — the candidate must not finish the causal account first but jump directly to the turn, then backfill the causes. Order inversion under pressure is a distinct skill from the canned three-parter.
- The "annotation quality" trap has been identified but not drilled: when pressed on weak rows, the tempting improvisation is to blame gold-label inconsistency — which contradicts the κ = 0.78 agreement story from the annotation slides and hands the panel a deck-internal inconsistency. The discipline: weak-row explanations come only from the documented causes (support, variability, ambiguity); never improvise a new weakness to explain an old one. This belongs on the slide-20 (error analysis) preparation list too, where the temptation will be stronger.
- The threshold-tuning answer (§4.7 — trade precision for recall per use case) has not been stress-tested against the follow-up "so why didn't you tune for higher recall if misses matter?" The honest answer: the default calibration matches the primary consumer (review workflow), the knob exists for other consumers, and recall-first tuning was evaluated qualitatively but a full per-use-case threshold study is future work. Not yet rehearsed.
- The pivot into slide 18 frames the ablation as *proof* ("to prove the loss-function choice was the right one"). This raises the evidentiary bar for slide 18 deliberately — the candidate has now promised proof twice (slide 12 planted the claim, slide 17 promises the proof). The slide 18 lesson must deliver the four-configuration table with the same numerical fluency discipline as slides 16–17, plus the significance machinery (3 seeds, ±0.4 pp, 25× ratio, paired bootstrap p < 0.01). The +11 F1 number — reserved since slide 12, the deck's longest-held reservation — finally lands there.

## Note on the evidence-section arc so far

Slide 16 established the evaluation methodology and deposited trust (two self-demotions, volunteered leakage concession). Slide 17 spends some of that trust (the per-entity claims land without re-arguing strict scoring or split design) and deposits more (the undefended REGION/CASUALTIES difference, the owned weak rows). Slide 18 is the arc's climax — the most-reserved number in the deck lands as the empirical answer to the modelling research question. The rehearsal priority across 16 → 17 → 18 is cumulative numerical fluency: four numbers (16), then eight rows (17), then a four-by-four ablation table (18). Each slide's recital is harder than the last, and stumbling on a later one retroactively cheapens the earlier ones. Drill them as a single sequence at least once before the live defence.

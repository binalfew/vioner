# Competitive-positioning delivery — acknowledge then position

Slide 6 introduces a new delivery skill: stating that prior systems (ACLED, UCDP, ICEWS, GDELT, Masakhane, AfriBERTa, LLMs) don't fit the use case *without sounding dismissive of them*. This matters because defence panels often include members who have worked with one of those systems — a dismissive tone toward Masakhane or GDELT can turn a benign panellist hostile within seconds.

## Implications

- The "acknowledge then position" pattern (state what the system does well, then state what gap it leaves) is the safe pattern for every comparative claim on slides 6, 7, and Q&A.
- The four-LLM-disqualifier list (closed weights, per-call cost, no auditability, data sovereignty) is critical — it is the answer to the "why not GPT-4?" question that almost every defence asks.
- The four-adjective closing ("African, automated, open, operational") is the hero line of slide 6 — it maps directly to gaps left by the systems above, so delivery must signal awareness of which gap each adjective closes.

## What this does not yet establish

- The user has not yet defended a question about LLMs under live pressure. The lesson includes the prepared answer; whether they can hold it under hostile questioning is unknown.

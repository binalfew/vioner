# Operational framing + the "independently necessary" defence

Slide 7 introduces two new craft skills. First, *operational framing* — stating problems in terms of what the user (the analyst) can't do, rather than what the technology is missing. Second, the *"independently necessary"* argument — defending why this is a four-part contribution rather than a single contribution, by showing that dropping any one gap leaves the analyst with an unusable system.

## Implications

- Operational framing is the rhetoric that signals "I understand the user, not just the technology." Every later contribution slide (10-14) benefits if slide 7 establishes this framing.
- The "independently necessary" defence is the structural backbone of the integration claim from slide 2 — it converts the abstract "integration is the contribution" thread into a concrete justification with four gaps that map 1:1 to four contributions.
- The candidate now has, in sequence: slide 1 (composure + headline plant) → slide 2 (integration thread) → slide 5 (why technically hard) → slide 6 (empty cell positioning) → slide 7 (four operational gaps). The arc from "the problem is real" to "and here's exactly what's missing" is fully constructed by slide 7's end.

## What this does not yet establish

- The candidate has not been drilled on holding the "why four and not three?" answer under hostile follow-up (e.g., "couldn't gaps 1 and 4 collapse into one?"). The lesson includes prepared answers; live drill is future work.

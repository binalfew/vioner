# Shift from technical defence to presentation craft

The user has shifted focus from defending technical content (focal loss, ablation) to *presenting* a specific slide (slide 2 — roadmap). This is a different category of skill: stagecraft, pacing, eye contact, and the structural choice of *what to say* and *what to leave for later*.

## Implications

- Lessons on individual slides should teach delivery mechanics (pauses, eye contact, what NOT to say) alongside the verbatim script.
- For slide 2 specifically, the most important payload is the *integration through-line* — the strategic claim that frames the rest of the talk. The lesson must make that visible as the key sentence, not just one line among many.
- Future lessons on other slides should follow the same pattern: visual of the slide, verbatim script in beats, what NOT to do, timed drill, quiz on the strategic move.

## What this does not yet establish

- The user has not demonstrated they can deliver the roadmap script under time pressure. The timed drill in this lesson is the first opportunity.
- No evidence yet on how the user handles audience-facing mechanics (eye contact, body language) — those are real-world skills that require dry-run in front of someone.

# The honest-metric reframe + numerical fluency as the evidence-section discipline

Slide 16 opens the evidence section (slides 16–19) and introduces the craft skill that governs it: the **honest-metric reframe**. When a slide contains a high number that is misleading (96.7 % token accuracy) and a lower number that is honest (0.909 micro F1), the candidate demotes the high number *themselves*, before the panel can. The demotion runs on the **degenerate-model test**: any metric a know-nothing model scores well on is not measuring learning — predict-O-everywhere scores 78 % accuracy and 0.0 F1, so the two metrics maximally disagree about a model that learned nothing, and F1 is the one telling the truth. Slide 16 performs this reframe *twice* (accuracy → F1, and relaxed → strict span scoring, declining a free +1.5–2 F1), which makes it the deck's credibility anchor: trust earned here is spent by slides 17 and 18.

## Implications

- The honest-metric reframe is the honest-middle binary pattern (slides 9–14) turned inward. Previous slides named and refused dismissive readings of *contributions*; slide 16 names and refuses an inflated reading of the candidate's *own results*. The posture statement: the candidate polices their own numbers harder than the panel would. This generalises to every results discussion — slide 17 (precision > recall on every row, framed as the *right* trade rather than hidden), slide 18 (seed variance volunteered alongside gains), slide 19 (the 4.0/5 lowest Likert item volunteered as the future-work driver). Each evidence slide should volunteer its own least-flattering number.
- The degenerate-model test is whiteboard-deployable and generalises beyond accuracy-vs-F1. Its shape: "construct the model that learned nothing; check what your metric gives it; any metric that rewards it is broken." The candidate can run this test live against any metric a panellist proposes. It is also the cleanest two-sentence answer in the deck — short enough to deliver under interruption.
- Numerical fluency is the evidence-section competence signal, and it has a specific spoken discipline: full decimal precision, digit by digit ("zero-point-nine-zero-nine"), no hedging vocabulary ("about", "around", "roughly", "I think"). Hedged numbers are the section's most common credibility leak — they read as the candidate not knowing their own results. This extends the numbers-and-acronyms delivery challenge identified at slide 3 to the highest-stakes numbers in the deck. The drill: three consecutive clean runs of Beat 2 with zero hedges.
- The 78 % O figure now does double duty across the deck: on slide 12 it motivated the training recipe (the imbalance the rebalancers attack); on slide 16 it motivates the metric choice (the imbalance that makes accuracy meaningless). One number, two duties, and the candidate should *name* the echo if asked — re-using load-bearing numbers across slides is deck-wide coherence the panel can audit, like the "integration evaluated end-to-end" verbatim callback between slides 14 and 15.
- The micro-vs-macro gap has a rehearsed *direction reading*: micro (0.909) exceeds macro (0.887) because the three highest-support entities (ACTOR 47.6 k, CITY 44.4 k, DATE 31.9 k spans) also have the three highest F1s — which is the reassuring direction, since analysts see those entities most often. The inverted relationship (micro < macro) would be the warning sign. Most candidates explain the gap mechanically and stop; the direction reframe converts an apparent inconsistency into evidence of operational fitness. This "read the gap's direction out loud" move likely generalises to slide 17 (precision > recall on every row → the right trade for analyst review).
- The benchmark-contextualisation move has a fixed claim-calibration: name the canonical benchmark (CoNLL-2003 ~0.92), name the difficulty delta (4 entities/mild imbalance vs 8 entities/severe imbalance), claim exactly "competitive on a harder distribution" — never "better" (false: 0.909 < 0.92), never "state of the art" (unverifiable). The comparison's job is reference frame, not leaderboard victory. Escalation path if pressed: backup B9 (0.94 vs 0.82 macro on the four entity types shared with generic BERT NER) — second reserve, behind the CoNLL first reserve.
- The reservation graph gained slide-16 entries: per-entity F1s (DATE 0.956 → VICTIM 0.817) are reserved for slide 17; +11 F1 remains reserved for slide 18; the CoNLL comparison and B9 are *conditional* reserves on slide 16 itself (deployed only against "is 0.909 good?"). One sanctioned partial release: if a panellist forces the "lucky seed" question, the 25× gain-to-noise ratio may name the +11 in passing — the reliability framing, not the ablation story.

## What this does not yet establish

- The candidate has not drilled the leakage answer's three defences in order under pressure. The order is strength-ranked — article-level split (structural), hash dedup (near-duplicate), template-pool partition (augmentation-specific) — and scrambling it under interruption weakens the answer. The third defence is the one a panel won't expect a candidate to have thought of; dropping it loses the most credit. A focused drill: deliver all three in order, then the volunteered §6.13 wire-copy concession, in under 30 seconds, three times.
- The whiteboard F1 chain (TP/FP/FN → precision → recall → harmonic mean → the 100-VICTIM worked example with TP 82 / FP 8 / FN 18) has been documented but not reproduced from memory. If a panellist says "show me how you compute F1", fumbling the arithmetic on the candidate's own metric would be disproportionately damaging on this slide specifically. Drill: full chain on paper from memory in under 60 seconds.
- The "why epoch 2?" follow-up is only partially pre-loaded. The data exists (epoch 2 = lowest val loss 0.0074 AND highest macro F1 0.887; epochs 3–4 show train loss falling while val loss rises — the overfitting signature; early-stopping rule = no val-loss improvement for 2 consecutive epochs), but the candidate has not rehearsed connecting it to the "isn't 2 epochs suspiciously short?" insinuation. The honest answer: BERT fine-tuning on a 40 k-example corpus converges fast; 2–4 epochs is the standard fine-tuning range (Devlin et al. recommend 2–4); short convergence is expected, not suspicious.
- The harmonic-mean trap question — "why not just average precision and recall?" — has no rehearsed answer. The arithmetic mean of P=1.0, R=0.1 is 0.55; the harmonic mean is ~0.18. The harmonic mean punishes one-sided models, which is the point: a model that finds almost nothing but is always right when it does should not score "average". This is a one-line whiteboard answer worth having loaded for a methodology-savvy panel.
- The "96.7 % accuracy vs 78 % baseline is still a 19-point lift — isn't accuracy informative after all?" counter-attack is unaddressed. The honest answer: the lift is real but compressed (the metric's ceiling effects make 19 points hard to interpret), and per-class behaviour is invisible inside it — accuracy cannot distinguish a model that nails VICTIM from one that ignores it, which is exactly the distinction this thesis is about. F1 makes the rare-class behaviour visible; that visibility is the requirement, not the convention. Worth one rehearsal pass before the live defence.

## Note on the structural role of slide 16 in the evidence section

Slide 16 is where the panel decides whether to trust the candidate's evaluation methodology wholesale. The two self-demotions (accuracy → F1, relaxed → strict) plus the volunteered leakage concession (§6.13) are trust deposits; slides 17 and 18 withdraw against them — per-entity claims and ablation gains land without re-arguing the methodology only if slide 16 established it. This means slide 16 under-rehearsed is a deck-wide risk, not a single-slide risk: a fumbled metric defence here forces slides 17–18 to re-litigate evaluation honesty with a now-skeptical panel. Rehearsal allocation should weight slide 16 accordingly — it is the cheapest slide to deliver (four numbers, two reframes, 75 seconds) but among the most expensive to deliver badly.

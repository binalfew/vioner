# The synthesis-plus-extension claim shape + the figure-walk technique

Slide 11 introduces two transferable craft skills. First, the *synthesis-plus-extension* claim — the defensive shape for any contribution that builds on multiple prior frameworks: name three sources, name a concrete extension, and name the wedge (the case prior frameworks misclassify). Second, the *figure walk* technique — how to deliver a complex tree visualisation in 30 seconds without enumerating the leaves: root → families → two examples per family → state Level-3 count + location, reserved.

## Implications

- The synthesis-plus-extension shape generalises directly to slide 14 (KB construction — synthesises curated armed-group lists and adds African coverage that public lists don't have) and to slide 18 (training recipe — synthesises focal loss and inverse-frequency weights and adds the combination ablation). Every contribution slide that builds on multiple prior techniques can use this shape: *cite three sources, name a specific extension, name the wedge*.
- The figure walk pattern (L0 → L1 → L2 with leaves *reserved*) extends to slide 13 (system architecture diagram) and slide 14 (end-to-end pipeline). Both are figure-centric slides where the temptation to dive into low-level detail would burn time. The same disciplinary move applies: state the count, state the location, never enumerate live.
- The "not a relabelling" denial is structurally identical to slide 10's "didn't get smaller as a concession" — the candidate is now drilling a consistent rhetorical pattern across the contribution arc: *name the dismissive reading inside your own sentence, refuse it, replace it with a precise positive claim*. This pattern transfers to every contribution slide; the panel never gets to name the reductive reading first.
- The two-source vs three-source distinction matters. Slide 9 reserved three numbers (κ, 212k→50k, +11 F1); slide 11 reserves three sources (ACLED, UCDP, PMVE). Three is the magic number for the deck — large enough to signal breadth, small enough to memorise and recite under pressure.
- Pastoralist–farmer clashes and cattle raiding are now the **canonical concrete extensions** the candidate uses to prove African specificity. Future slides that need to claim African specificity (KB, dataset, problem framing) should reach for the same two examples rather than inventing fresh ones; consistency across the deck strengthens recall.

## What this does not yet establish

- The candidate has not been live-drilled on the source attribution under interruption. If a panellist asks "which specific part of UCDP did you draw from?", the answer (state-based / non-state / one-sided distinction) needs to come within 5 seconds. A future drill should rehearse single-word probe → single-sentence specific attribution.
- The κ = 0.76 L3 validation number is held in reserve but hasn't been tested against the harder follow-up: "how did you decide the L3 granularity was right rather than L2 or L4?" The answer (operational analytics layer needed L3 to disaggregate situation reports from incident triage) is in the thesis but the candidate hasn't practised deploying it under time pressure.
- The "what about ACLED's sub-event types?" attack is unaddressed. ACLED does have a second-level "sub-event type" column; a sceptical panellist may push that the synthesis claim is overstated because ACLED already covers some of the depth claimed. The canonical answer (ACLED sub-event types are flat, not hierarchical, and don't include the Communal extensions) is the next defence layer to build.
- The L1 family enumeration is rhythmically tricky — four names in a row needs deliberate pauses to avoid sounding like a comma-separated list. The lesson teaches the rhythm but muscle-memory drill is future work. A focused drill: deliver "Political. Criminal. Communal. State." with a one-second pause between each, three times in a row, until the rhythm is internalised.

The four-level hierarchical taxonomy

1 · Where this slide sits in the arc

Slide 10 promised that the dropped EVENT_TYPE would be recovered downstream by a taxonomy classifier. Slide 11 makes good on that promise — it shows what the taxonomy is. The slide is the second of the three contribution slides that, together, close the loop on RQ1 (schema) and tee up RQ2 (model). Without slide 11, the slide-10 reframe has an unpaid IOU.

The function of slide 11. It earns the "recovered downstream" claim from slide 10 by making the recovery architecture visible. The panel cannot accept "EVENT_TYPE → taxonomy classifier" as a serious answer unless they can see the taxonomy. Slide 11 puts it on screen — synthesised from three reference frameworks, extended with two African-specific categories none of them cover.

What the panel walks in with

  • ACLED is the prior they know. Most panel members will have seen ACLED's six top-level categories before. Their first instinct will be to map this taxonomy onto ACLED and ask "is this just ACLED with different labels?"
  • Taxonomies trigger pedantry. A four-level tree with ~95 leaves invites detailed scrutiny ("why is X under Y rather than Z?"). Don't open that door on stage. Reserve the leaves to Annex B.
  • "African-specific" needs evidence. A claim that this taxonomy is African-tailored only lands if you can name concrete categories that prior frameworks don't have. Pastoralist-farmer and communal cattle raiding are those categories.

Beat 1 — name and frame ~10 seconds
"The taxonomy gives the event categories that the slide-10 schema deliberately doesn't tag. Four levels. Root, four families, sub-categories, and roughly ninety-five terminal leaves at Level 3."
Open with the callback to slide 10 — "the slide-10 schema deliberately doesn't tag" earns the previous slide's reframe by completing the recovery. Name the depth ("four levels") in the same breath as the leaf count, then move on. Do not enumerate the leaves.
2
Beat 2 — walk the figure, root → families → examples ~30 seconds
"At the root, Violent Events. The four Level-1 families on the next row: Political — armed conflict, riots; Criminal — banditry, kidnapping; Communal — pastoralist–farmer clashes, cattle raiding; State — repression, forced disappearance. Each family has eight to fifteen Level-2 children. Level 3 holds the operational categories — about ninety-five in total, in Annex B."
Walk the figure in a strict L0 → L1 → L2 path. Two Level-2 examples per family is enough; six examples would burn the time budget. The Communal pair (pastoralist–farmer, cattle raiding) is delivered with slightly more weight — those are the extensions; you want the panel to notice.
3
Beat 3 — the synthesis-plus-extension claim ~25 seconds
"The structure synthesises three existing frameworks — ACLED, UCDP, and PMVE. The contribution is the extension: pastoralist–farmer clashes and communal cattle raiding are not present at this granularity in any of the three sources. ACLED has a 'Violence against civilians' bucket, but pastoralist–farmer disputes don't sit cleanly there — they're driven by resource competition, not political targeting. The extensions are where this taxonomy earns its African specificity."
Two-part move. (1) Cite the synthesis sources — saying "ACLED, UCDP, PMVE" by name signals you've surveyed prior work and aren't claiming novelty where there isn't any. (2) Name the extensions concretely. The pastoralist–farmer wedge ("driven by resource competition, not political targeting") is the line that proves it's a real category gap, not just a finer label.
4
Beat 4 — the hero reframe ~15 seconds
"This is not a relabelling of ACLED. It's a synthesis with African-specific extensions ACLED doesn't reach."
The "not a relabelling" denial is load-bearing because it's the exact attack the panel is most likely to bring. Naming the attack inside your own sentence neutralises it. Pause before "ACLED doesn't reach" — let the contrast land. This is the slide's eight-word memory anchor.

4 · The synthesis-plus-extension claim — the core skill

Slide 11's central craft skill is the synthesis-plus-extension claim: the rhetorical shape used to defend any contribution that builds on multiple prior frameworks. The shape has two compulsory parts:

  1. Name the sources you synthesised. Three named sources beats "we drew on the literature." Specificity is what makes the synthesis claim survive scrutiny.
  2. Name a concrete extension that prior sources don't have. Vague extensions ("better for the African context") don't land. Specific extensions ("pastoralist–farmer clashes") do.

If you only claim synthesis

"The taxonomy combines insights from ACLED, UCDP, and PMVE."

"It's tailored to the African context."

→ Reads as: relabelling. The panel will ask "what's actually new?"

Synthesis + concrete extension

"Synthesises ACLED, UCDP, and PMVE — and adds pastoralist–farmer clashes and communal cattle raiding, which none cover at this granularity."

"Pastoralist–farmer is resource competition, not political targeting — ACLED's nearest bucket misclassifies it."

→ Reads as: principled construction with a named contribution.

The three sources, named so you can deploy them

Source frameworks — synthesised, not copied

ACLEDArmed Conflict Location & Event Data Project. The dominant taxonomy in the field — six top-level event types. This taxonomy uses ACLED's structural backbone but extends the Communal branch.
UCDPUppsala Conflict Data Program. State-based / non-state / one-sided distinction at the actor-pair level. Contributes the State-violence branch's internal structure.
PMVEPolitically Motivated Violent Events — the prior typology for ideological / political violence. Contributes the Political branch's Level-2 distinctions.

The two extensions, with their evidence

Extension 1

Pastoralist–farmer clashes

Resource-competition violence between mobile herders and settled farmers, especially across the Sahel. ACLED's nearest bucket is "Violence against civilians" — but that misclassifies the bilateral nature of the dispute.

Extension 2

Communal cattle raiding

Inter-clan livestock theft, often armed, common in East African pastoral zones. ACLED treats this as criminal violence, but the communal-identity dimension is operationally distinct — the responding analyst needs to know which clan, not just that a theft occurred.

5 · Walking the figure on stage

The slide is figure-centric. The panel's eyes will track the tree top-down regardless of what you say; your job is to give them a structured commentary that matches their visual sweep. The walk technique:

The L0 → L1 → L2 walk — never enumerate L3

1Name the root. "Violent Events." One word, one beat. Establishes the scope of the figure.
2Name the four Level-1 families. "Political. Criminal. Communal. State." Said as four distinct names with brief pauses between — not as a comma-separated list. The four families are the structural skeleton of the contribution.
3Pair each family with two Level-2 examples. Two per family is the rhythm. More than two burns time; one per family makes the families look thin.
4State the Level-3 count and reserve the detail. "About ninety-five terminal leaves at Level 3, in Annex B." Number plus location. Never enumerate Level-3 categories live — that's the trap.
5Land the synthesis + extension claim. Beat 3 + Beat 4 — three named sources, two named extensions, one hero line.
The trap. If a panellist asks "what's at Level 3 under [family X]?" — answer with the count of leaves under that family ("about twenty-five under Political") and then defer to Annex B or backup slide B5. Don't try to recite L3 categories from memory; the room will hear hesitation as uncertainty about the taxonomy itself, which is the exact opposite of what slide 11 is doing.

7 · Three pre-loaded interrupts

"Is this just ACLED with different labels?"
"No — for two named reasons. First, the synthesis: the structural backbone draws on ACLED, but the State branch's internal structure comes from UCDP's state-based / non-state / one-sided distinction, and the Political branch's Level-2 distinctions come from PMVE. Second, the extension: pastoralist–farmer clashes and communal cattle raiding are Level-2 categories that none of the three sources cover at this granularity. ACLED's nearest bucket for pastoralist–farmer is 'Violence against civilians', which misclassifies the bilateral resource-competition nature of the dispute. So: ACLED's backbone, three-source synthesis, two African-specific extensions. Not a relabelling."
The key three-part move: (1) name the synthesis sources, (2) name the extensions concretely, (3) name the wedge ("ACLED's nearest bucket misclassifies"). All three together neutralise the relabelling charge.
"How did you validate that ninety-five Level-3 categories are the right granularity?"
"Two validations. First, coverage: the L3 set was constructed bottom-up by annotating two hundred randomly sampled ACLED events and assigning each to the most specific category that fit; the leaves that emerged from that exercise are the ninety-five. Second, agreement: when a second annotator independently classified a hundred events to the L3 leaves, the kappa was point seven six. Annex B has the full leaves and the validation protocol."
Concrete numbers — 200 sampled events, κ = 0.76 on L3 — make the validation claim survive. Without numbers, "we validated it" sounds like assertion. With numbers, it sounds like measurement.
"Why use a hierarchical taxonomy rather than flat event labels?"
"Three reasons. First, the production analytics layer aggregates at multiple granularities — coarse for situation reports, fine for incident triage. A flat taxonomy forces you to pick one granularity and stick with it. Second, the cross-source synthesis is only possible hierarchically — ACLED, UCDP, and PMVE disagree at Level 2 but agree at Level 1, so the hierarchy is what lets the synthesis hold together. Third, classifier failure modes are graceful — misclassifying within a family is far less damaging than misclassifying across families, and a hierarchical loss can penalise the two differently."
Three reasons, parallel structure (the slide-5 enumeration pattern). The third reason — graceful failure modes — is the technically deepest, and tells a panellist with ML expertise that you've thought about the loss-function implications of the structure.

8 · The hero close

The hero line — memorise verbatim "Not a relabelling — a synthesis with African-specific extensions ACLED doesn't reach."

Why this line is the hero

  • "Not a relabelling" names and refuses the attack the panel is most likely to bring. This is the same pattern as slide 10's "didn't get smaller as a concession" — name the objection inside your own sentence, neutralise it before it's asked.
  • "Synthesis with ... extensions" states the two compulsory parts of the claim shape: three named sources, two named extensions. Compact enough to remember, specific enough to stand up.
  • "ACLED doesn't reach" closes by naming the comparator the panel actually cares about. ACLED is the field's reference point; explicitly saying ACLED doesn't reach where this taxonomy does is the field-relative contribution claim.
  • Consistent with the deck's posture. Slide 9: measurement, not preference. Slide 10: didn't get smaller, got trustworthy. Slide 11: not a relabelling, an extension. The pattern is always name the dismissive reading, refuse it, replace it with a precise positive claim.

10 · Quiz

Q1. The panel asks: "Is this just ACLED with different labels?"

Pick the strongest response.

  • "No — the taxonomy is original work; ACLED was only used as a reference."
  • "No — it covers more granular categories than ACLED does."
  • "No, for two reasons. The structural backbone draws on ACLED but the Political branch's L2 distinctions come from PMVE and the State branch's structure from UCDP — that's the synthesis. And the Communal branch adds pastoralist–farmer clashes and cattle raiding, which none of the three sources cover at this granularity — that's the extension. ACLED's nearest bucket for pastoralist–farmer misclassifies the bilateral resource-competition nature of the dispute."
  • "No — ACLED is designed for global use, while this taxonomy is for Africa."

Q2. The panel asks: "What's at Level 3 under the Political branch?"

Pick the safest response.

  • "For example, IED attack, ambush, assassination, riot dispersal, armed clash, ..."
  • "There are about twenty-five Level-3 leaves under Political. The full list is in Annex B of the thesis, and backup slide B5 if you want to look at the tree now."
  • "I'd rather not list them all — there are quite a few."
  • "Roughly fifteen — I'd have to check the exact number."

Q3. Why does the script emphasise the Communal branch (pastoralist–farmer, cattle raiding) more than the Political branch?

Pick the strongest reason.

  • Because the Communal branch holds the two extensions that prove the taxonomy isn't just a relabelling of ACLED. Without the extensions, the "African-specific" claim has no evidence behind it.
  • Because pastoralist–farmer clashes are the most common event type in the dataset.
  • Because the Political branch is already well covered by ACLED, so it doesn't need defending.
  • Because Communal violence is the central topic of the thesis.

Q4. The taxonomy synthesises three frameworks. Which contributes the State-violence branch's internal structure?

Pick the correct source.

  • ACLED — the operational gold standard.
  • UCDP — Uppsala Conflict Data Program, with the state-based / non-state / one-sided distinction.
  • PMVE — Politically Motivated Violent Events.
  • All three contribute equally; no single source dominates any branch.

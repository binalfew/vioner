The grounded 8-entity schema — Contribution 1

The first contribution slide. The skill: turning "we dropped 18 of 26 entities" — which sounds like a weakening — into a strengthening claim, by routing every dropped entity through a downstream recovery path. The reframe is the entire defence.
Slide: 10 of 22 Target time: 100 seconds Hero line: "Reduction is a strengthening — every label has ground truth" Reserved cards: 80 % threshold · Nov 2025 pilot · EVENT_TYPE at 58 % · 17 BIO labels

1 · Where this slide sits in the arc

Slide 9 pre-stamped the methodology — every contribution that follows now inherits design-science legitimacy. Slide 10 is the first contribution slide, the schema loop from slide 9 unfolded with its own evidence. The panel is no longer asking "is this a real problem?"; they're now asking "is this contribution principled?". Slide 10's job is to convert "we dropped 18 entities" — which sounds like a contribution weakened — into "we retained only labels with verifiable ground truth" — which is a strengthening.

The function of slide 10. It answers RQ1 — the entity schema. It also establishes a rhetorical pattern that will be reused on slides 11–14: every contribution begins with a measurement (here, the grounding pilot), follows with a principled reduction (here, 26 → 8), and ends with a recovery story (here, the dropped types are not lost — they are recovered downstream via the KB and the taxonomy classifier). The pattern is: measure → reduce → recover.

What the panel walks in with

  • The proposal's 26 entities. They read your proposal six months ago; they expect 26. The first thing they see on this slide is "8 retained."
  • Suspicion that you scoped down. A reduction from 26 to 8 looks, at first glance, like the contribution shrank by 70 %.
  • Implicit benchmark. Most NER papers report on 4–7 entity types. 26 is unusually ambitious; 8 is in the normal range.

Beat 4 — the hero reframe — turns each of those three priors into an argument for the new schema. Don't apologise for the reduction; recast it.

The table has three columns. The retained column (green in your eyes) is the supervised schema — what the BERT model actually learns to tag. The dropped column (italic) is the schema that didn't survive the pilot. The arrows show that the dropped entities are not abandoned; they are routed to a downstream component that can handle them more reliably than a noisy NER head.

The arithmetic on the bottom row

8 entities × 2 (B-, I-) + 1 (O) = 17 BIO labels

Every entity becomes two labels in B-I-O encoding (Beginning, Inside, Outside). Plus the single Outside-of-any-entity label. That's the number the model's classification head actually outputs. If a panellist asks "why 17?", that's the arithmetic — be ready to say it cleanly.

3 · The 100-second script, in four beats

1
Beat 1 — name the RQ, set the original target ~15 seconds
"RQ1 — the schema. The original proposal called for twenty-six entity types — everything an analyst might care about: motive, intent, organisation, duration, weapons, geographic descriptors. Before committing the training compute, I needed to know which of those could actually be supervised."
Open by naming the RQ. The panel knows the proposal had 26 — saying it first removes the surprise. The framing "before committing the training compute" signals this was a planned validation step, not a retrospective excuse.
2
Beat 2 — the grounding pilot ~30 seconds
"In November 2025, I ran what I called a grounding pilot. A sample of articles was hand-annotated, and for each entity type I measured the fraction that could be located verbatim in the source text — that is, a phrase the annotator could point at, not a category they had to infer. Types below eighty percent grounding were dropped from the supervised schema. The reasoning: training on inferred labels would introduce systematic noise, because two annotators infer differently for the same article."
"Verbatim" is the load-bearing word. Slow down on it. The contrast "point at, not infer" is the operational distinction that makes the threshold defensible. This beat introduces the new term ("grounding pilot") — say it as if it's a recognised technique, because by the time you're using it as a noun, it is.
3
Beat 3 — walk the table, kept then recovered ~35 seconds
"What remained were eight entities, in the left column: ACTOR, VICTIM, ACTION, DATE, REGION, CITY, DISTRICT, CASUALTIES. In BIO encoding, that's seventeen labels. The dropped types on the right are not lost — they're recovered downstream. EVENT_TYPE is reconstructed by the taxonomy classifier from the action verb plus context. COUNTRY is recovered by a knowledge-base lookup from the most specific WHERE entity. So we get eight clean training signals plus two free downstream entities."
Walk the table vertically — left column first (the 8 retained, said as a fluent list), then the recovery story for the two most-asked-about drops (EVENT_TYPE and COUNTRY). Don't enumerate every dropped type — that wastes time. Concede the WHY drops (MOTIVE, TRIGGER) only if asked; they're noted as future work.
4
Beat 4 — the hero reframe ~20 seconds
"That trade — fewer entities, but every one with a verifiable ground-truth signal — was the single most consequential methodological choice of the thesis. The schema didn't get smaller as a concession. It got trustworthy."
This is the slide's hero line. The pivot is the phrase "didn't get smaller as a concession — it got trustworthy". That reframe converts the apparent loss (18 entities gone) into the gain (every retained label is auditable). Pause before "trustworthy" — let it land. This sentence is what the panel will remember from slide 10.

4 · The grounded-reduction reframe — the core skill

Slide 10's central craft skill is the grounded-reduction reframe: when a contribution looks smaller than what was proposed, recast the reduction as a strengthening of the remaining signal. This pattern works whenever you scoped down for a measurable reason. It is the rhetorical move that prevents the panel from reading scope reduction as weakness.

How it sounds if you apologise

"We had to drop eighteen entities because they were too hard."

"The schema is narrower than the proposal."

"MOTIVE and TRIGGER didn't work out."

→ Reads as: contribution shrank by 70 %.

How the reframe sounds

"The pilot measured grounding; types below 80 % were routed elsewhere."

"Every retained label has a verifiable ground-truth signal."

"Dropped types are recovered downstream by KB lookup or the taxonomy classifier."

→ Reads as: schema got more trustworthy, and the system still covers what was proposed.
The trap. Words that collapse the frame: "unfortunately", "we couldn't", "had to drop", "too noisy", "didn't work". Each of these signals concession. Replace with measurement language: "the pilot measured", "the grounding rate was", "the threshold routed". Reduction described as routing, not losing, is the reframe in one word.

Where dropped entities go — the recovery diagram

What "recovered downstream" means concretely

EVENT_TYPE→Taxonomy classifier at the segment level, fed by the ACTION verb plus context. Section 4.3 / slide 17.
COUNTRY→KB lookup from the most-specific WHERE entity (CITY → DISTRICT → REGION → COUNTRY). Section 4.5 / slide 22.
ORGANIZATION→KB lookup against the curated 150-armed-group list, anchored on the ACTOR span. Section 4.5 / slide 22.
MOTIVE, TRIGGER→Conceded as a future-work item. Not recoverable from text alone with current methods; requires causal inference layered on top of the extraction.

The recovery story is what closes the reframe. Without it, "we dropped 18" still sounds like loss. With it, "we routed 18 entities to higher-precision pathways" sounds like architectural decomposition. Decomposition, not deletion, is the operative word.

7 · Three pre-loaded interrupts

"You reduced the schema from 26 to 8 — isn't that a weakening of the contribution?"
"It's a strengthening. The proposal's 26 entities included types the analyst infers from context — MOTIVE, INTENT, ORGANIZATION sub-types — rather than reads from the article. Training a model on inferred labels introduces systematic noise, because two annotators infer differently. The grounding pilot in November measured this; types below eighty percent verbatim grounding were routed elsewhere. EVENT_TYPE goes to the taxonomy classifier. COUNTRY and ORGANIZATION go to the KB. Every retained label has verifiable ground truth — that's why the trained model is auditable. The schema isn't narrower; it's decomposed."
The key sentence: "the schema isn't narrower; it's decomposed." That word — decomposed — is the one-word reframe. Use it.
"How did you choose the eighty-percent threshold? It seems arbitrary."
"The threshold is the elbow of the grounding-rate distribution. When I plotted the per-entity grounding rates from the pilot, types clustered above eighty percent or below sixty percent — there was very little in between. Eighty was the natural cutoff in the data, not a value imposed before the pilot. It's reported as a design parameter rather than a discovered constant — if a future replication finds the elbow elsewhere, the methodology is unchanged, only the retained set shifts."
Two-part move: (1) the elbow argument grounds the number in the data; (2) "design parameter, not discovered constant" pre-empts the "what if I disagree with eighty?" follow-up. The methodology is robust to the threshold.
"What about MOTIVE? Surely the motive of a violent event matters to the analyst."
"It matters, and we don't pretend otherwise. The MOTIVE entity is almost never written verbatim in the article — analysts infer it from prior knowledge of the actor, the location, the political context. Training a BERT head on that inference would have been training on noise. So MOTIVE is conceded as a future-work item — natural-language QA layered on the extraction output, or a causal-inference component on top of the actor and action entities. The next layer of the architecture, not this layer."
Concede cleanly. Don't pretend MOTIVE can be recovered from the current architecture. The honest answer — "the next layer, not this layer" — preserves credibility for everything else on the slide.

8 · The hero close

The hero line — memorise verbatim "Fewer entities, but every one with verifiable ground truth. The schema didn't get smaller as a concession — it got trustworthy."

Why this line is the hero

  • "Didn't get smaller as a concession" directly names and refuses the framing the panel is most likely to bring. Naming an objection inside your own sentence neutralises it.
  • "It got trustworthy" closes on the operational value. "Trustworthy" is the word an analyst — or a panel member thinking about an analyst — actually wants to hear about a model.
  • Verifiable ground truth is the inheritance from slide 9's "measurement, not preference" hinge. The audit-ability claim doesn't appear from nowhere; it's the design-science loop closing on this specific contribution.
  • Internally consistent. Slide 4 framed analyst work as operational. Slide 7 framed gaps operationally. Slide 9 framed iteration empirically. Slide 10 frames the schema reduction as trustworthiness. The deck's posture stays consistent: external-evidence words, not internal-feeling words.

10 · Quiz

Q1. The panel asks: "Isn't reducing from 26 entities to 8 a weakening of the contribution?"

Pick the strongest response.

  • "Yes, but the eight remaining entities cover the most important 5W1H slots."
  • "The reduction was unavoidable — eighteen of the original entities turned out to be too noisy to train on."
  • "It's a strengthening. The pilot routed types below eighty-percent grounding to higher-precision pathways — EVENT_TYPE to the taxonomy classifier, COUNTRY to the KB — so every retained label has verifiable ground truth. The schema isn't narrower; it's decomposed."
  • "We're considering adding more entities in future work to address this concern."

Q2. Why is the live slide delivered without citing the 58 % EVENT_TYPE grounding rate?

Pick the strongest reason.

  • Because 58 % is too damaging to cite publicly.
  • The slide is about the contribution (the 8 grounded entities); listing per-entity grounding rates would shift panel attention to "is 58 % really that low?" instead of "is this schema principled?". Reserving the number lets you deploy it on demand and signals the pilot had richer evidence than shown.
  • Per-entity numbers don't belong in defence presentations.
  • The 58 % figure is reported on a later slide instead.

Q3. The slide says "8 entities → 17 BIO labels." Where does 17 come from?

Pick the correct arithmetic.

  • 8 × 2 (B- and I- per entity) + 1 (the single O label) = 17.
  • 8 + 9 padding labels for class balance = 17.
  • The original 26 minus 9 future-work items = 17.
  • 17 is the BERT tokeniser's default label count.

Q4. The panel asks: "How did you choose eighty percent as the threshold?"

Pick the strongest response.

  • "It's a standard threshold in the NER literature."
  • "Eighty percent felt like a reasonable cutoff."
  • "We tried several thresholds and eighty was the best for our metrics."
  • "The threshold is the elbow of the grounding-rate distribution — types clustered above eighty or below sixty, with little in between. It's reported as a design parameter, not a discovered constant; if a future replication finds the elbow elsewhere, the methodology is unchanged."

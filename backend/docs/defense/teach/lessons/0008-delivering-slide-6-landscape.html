Lesson 08 · Slide 6 — Competitive landscape

Defeat "why not just use GPT-4?" in 90 seconds

Slide 6 is the most-anticipated slide of the talk — every panel asks the LLM question. This lesson teaches you the four-disqualifier answer AND the acknowledge-then-position pattern that lets you state competitors don't fit without sounding dismissive of legitimate prior work.

Mission link. Land "VioNER occupies the empty cell" + pre-empt the LLM attack. Skill type. Competitive positioning without dismissal. Time. 16 minutes.

Lesson 7 taught you to deliver four technical challenges in parallel. Slide 6 does the next move: positions VioNER against six categories of prior work, naming systems specifically (ACLED, UCDP, ICEWS, GDELT, Masakhane, AfriBERTa, LLMs), and lands the "empty cell" claim that justifies the contribution. The delivery risk is high — every defence panel includes someone who has worked with one of those systems. A dismissive tone toward Masakhane or GDELT turns a benign panellist hostile within seconds.

The four-item disqualifier. Slow your pace through the list — each ground gets a beat. "Non-starter for many AU member states" is the geopolitical close that no panellist will challenge.

Four adjectives. End on "operational" with a clean stop. Pause one beat. Advance to slide 7. This is the strategic punchline — give it weight.

3 · The "acknowledge then position" pattern

This is the central craft skill of slide 6. Every competitor mention follows the same two-clause structure: acknowledge what they do well, then position the gap they leave. Never just dismiss.

SystemAcknowledge (what they do well)Position (what gap they leave)
ACLED, UCDPgold standardhand-coded — they are the TARGET, not competitors
ICEWS, GDELTautomatedgeneric CAMEO schema, closed or noisy
Generic NER(implicit — known general competence)doesn't know African armed groups
Masakhane, AfriBERTaAfrican NLP coveragestops at the model boundary, no operational packaging
LLMs (GPT-4, Claude)(implicit — general capability assumed)disqualified on four operational grounds
The pattern in one rule. Never make a comparative claim without acknowledging what the other system does well first. "Closed or noisy" follows "automated"; "no operational packaging" follows "African NLP"; "TARGET, not competitor" follows "gold standard". The acknowledge clause inoculates against any panellist who has worked with that system.

4 · The four LLM disqualifiers — the answer to "why not GPT-4?"

Almost every defence asks this question. The script answers it in the slide itself, but you should also have it ready as a stand-alone answer for the Q&A.

  1. Closed weights — the vendor can update silently and break our reproducibility.
  2. Per-call cost — scales with volume; 30,000 articles/year × per-call fee is a recurring budget line.
  3. No auditability — we can't trace the inference path. For a system feeding AU early-warning decisions, this is a non-negotiable.
  4. Data sovereignty — sending conflict metadata to a US commercial API is a non-starter for many AU member states.
These are operational grounds, not technical. You're not saying LLMs are bad — you're saying they don't fit the operational requirements of this use case. That framing keeps even pro-LLM panellists on side.

The "operational, not technical" framing

Critically: the script does not say "LLMs don't perform well on this task" or "LLMs are inferior". It says "LLMs are disqualified on four operational grounds". The candidate is making an institutional fit argument, not a quality argument. This is essential — many panellists use LLMs daily for other tasks and would react badly to a quality dismissal.

7 · Three pre-loaded interrupts

"Why not just use GPT-4 or Claude directly?"
"Four operational disqualifiers. Closed weights — the vendor can update silently and break reproducibility. Per-call cost — scales with volume; thirty thousand articles a year becomes a recurring budget line. No auditability — we can't trace the inference path, which is non-negotiable for a system feeding AU early-warning decisions. Data sovereignty — sending conflict metadata to a US commercial API is a non-starter for many AU member states. LLMs remain a credible future baseline to compare against, but not a substitute for an auditable on-prem system."
The closing concession — "credible future baseline to compare against" — keeps you respectful of LLMs while holding the position. Use it.
"Doesn't Masakhane already solve African NER?"
"Masakhane is excellent for African-language coverage — translation, language ID, basic NER on African languages. But it stops at the model boundary. VioNER goes further: violent-event-specific entities like the perpetrator-versus-victim distinction, KB validation against world facts, taxonomy classification, and a deployable web platform. We use the same family of pretrained models; we add the operational layer."
"Stops at the model boundary" is the key phrase. Concede generously on what Masakhane does well; the gap is operational, not technical.
"How does VioNER compare against ICEWS or GDELT in coverage?"
"Direct comparison isn't possible — ICEWS uses CAMEO event codes, VioNER uses 5W1H structured records. Different output schemas, so head-to-head F1 isn't meaningful. Backup B9 compares against generic BERT NER models on the entity-tag overlap. The right framing isn't "VioNER beats ICEWS" — it's "VioNER produces a different shape of output that the analyst actually needs."
Refuse the head-to-head framing politely. The "different shape of output" reframe is the move.

8 · Timed practice

Six beats, ~90 seconds target. Range: 80-100 seconds with internal pacing.

0.0 s
The harder rep. Cover the slide. Recite the six beats in order from memory: opener · ACLED/UCDP · ICEWS/GDELT · Generic+Masakhane · LLM-four · empty-cell-four-adjectives. If you swap ICEWS/GDELT with ACLED/UCDP order, or forget that ACLED+UCDP get the "TARGET not competitor" framing, you need another pass. The order matters — gold-standard first, then automated-but-wrong-shape, then research-only, then disqualified, then VioNER.

9 · Quiz

Q1. A panellist asks: "Why not just use GPT-4?"

Pick the strongest first-sentence opening to your answer.

  • "LLMs aren't actually that good at NER on African content."
  • "Four operational disqualifiers — closed weights, per-call cost, no auditability, and data sovereignty."
  • "GPT-4 is expensive, and we don't have the budget to use it."
  • "LLMs can't be fine-tuned to our specific schema."

Q2. Why does the script say "ACLED is the TARGET of this work, not a competitor"?

Pick the strategic function.

  • Because ACLED is too well-respected to criticise.
  • Because the candidate trained on ACLED data.
  • It disarms any ACLED-sympathetic panellist by repositioning ACLED as the institution the work serves, not the system it competes with. Even if your work surpasses ACLED's throughput, you frame yourself as supporting their mission.
  • Because ACLED is hand-coded and therefore not a fair comparison.

Q3. The four-adjective closing — "African, automated, open, and operational" — is doing what?

Pick the correct reading.

  • Listing VioNER's general qualities for marketing purposes.
  • Stating the four contributions of the thesis.
  • Describing the four-axis evaluation criteria for NER systems.
  • Each adjective references a specific gap left by the systems just discussed: African→Generic NER, Automated→ACLED, Open→LLMs, Operational→Masakhane. Four checkmarks against four gaps.

10 · Fluency checklist

  • I pronounce ACLED as "ACK-led", GDELT as "GEE-delt" (hard G), Masakhane as "ma-sa-KAH-nay".
  • I say "TARGET, not competitor" for ACLED with clear emphasis on "TARGET".
  • I deliver the four LLM disqualifiers in order — closed weights · per-call cost · no auditability · data sovereignty — without scrambling.
  • I land "non-starter for many AU member states" as the geopolitical close, not as a flat statement.
  • I deliver the four-adjective closing — "African, automated, open, and operational" — like four checkmarks, not a recital.
  • I never dismiss a system without first acknowledging what it does well (Masakhane → "African NLP coverage", ACLED → "gold standard").
  • I have the "LLMs are a credible future baseline" concession ready in case the panel pushes back on the disqualifier list.
  • My last timed rep was between 80 and 100 seconds with internal pacing.
If all eight are ticked, slide 6 is armoured. You have neutralised the "why not GPT-4?" attack before it leaves the panellist's mouth, and you have positioned VioNER without making enemies of any prior-work community.

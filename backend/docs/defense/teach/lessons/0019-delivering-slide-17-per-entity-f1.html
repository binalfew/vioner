4 · The operational translation — the core skill

The reliability map is the move that converts a metrics table into a usability claim. Each F1 band maps to an analyst behaviour. This framing is why the slide exists — without it, slide 17 is "more numbers after slide 16"; with it, slide 17 is the answer to "what is it actually like to use this system?"

Trust — skim only

DATE 0.956 · CITY 0.934

Essentially solved. Date expressions and named cities follow recognisable patterns. Analyst: "I never have to add a missing date."

Spot-check

ACTOR 0.923 · REGION 0.891

Mostly solved. Actors: check for new groups not yet in training data or KB. Regions: occasional region/country confusion.

Verify the detail

CASUALTIES 0.885 · ACTION 0.866

Counts mostly right — verify the qualifier ("at least" sometimes dropped). Actions: active voice caught; passive constructions ("were ambushed") sometimes need adding.

Correct — read carefully

DISTRICT 0.826 · VICTIM 0.817

The two fields needing real analyst attention. Districts confused with cities/regions; victims have extreme phrasing variability.

The operational-translation move — three steps, reusable for any metrics table

1Band the numbers. Eight decimals collapse into four behavioural tiers: trust / spot-check / verify / correct. Nobody remembers eight numbers; everyone remembers four instructions.
2Phrase each tier as what the analyst does, not what the model scores. "Spot-check for new groups" is an instruction; "0.923" is a statistic. The instruction is what the panel's institutional consumers (AU-CEWS, ACLED) would actually act on.
3Connect to the workflow claim. The map is what makes review-not-rewrite save time: the analyst knows what to skim and what to read carefully. Without the map, the analyst re-reads everything and the time saving evaporates.

5 · Precision > recall on every row — read the direction out loud

This is slide 16's "read the gap's direction" move applied a second time. The uniform pattern — precision above recall on all eight rows — is not an accident to gloss over but a designed behaviour to narrate.

The three-step reading. (1) The mechanism: when uncertain, the model defaults to O — the safe choice. Misses some real entities (lower recall), raises fewer false alarms (higher precision). (2) The direction is right for this consumer: for an analyst reviewing extracted records, a false alarm costs a deletion plus eroded trust; a miss costs an addition the analyst was scanning for anyway. Fewer false alarms wins. (3) And it's tunable: the per-category confidence thresholds (pipeline step 5, slide 15) can trade precision for recall per use case — a recall-hungry consumer can lower the thresholds. Designed trade, stated openly, adjustable knob: that is a complete defence.

6 · Weakest-number ownership — the VICTIM account

VICTIM 0.817 is the lowest number the panel will see until the error-analysis slide. The discipline: you bring up why, with a causal account in three parts, before anyone asks. An owned weakness is context; a discovered weakness is a finding.

The three-part VICTIM account

CAUSE 1Low support. 5,492 gold spans vs 47,612 for ACTOR — nearly nine times less training signal. Rare entities have less to learn from and higher evaluation variance.
CAUSE 2Extreme phrasing variability. Anything can be a victim: "civilians", "Christian worshippers", "the bus driver's family", "internally displaced schoolgirls". Compare DATE, where a handful of patterns covers nearly everything. Few examples × wide variety is the hardest learning setting in the schema.
CONTEXT0.817 is the lifted number. Plain cross-entropy puts VICTIM at 0.708 — the training recipe bought back eleven points. The remaining gap is structural noise needing real-news expansion (limitation 2) or span-level CRF boundary refinement (future work 4). Deploy the 0.708→0.817 framing only if asked here — the full ablation story is slide 18's.
And the DISTRICT account, one breath. DISTRICT 0.826 is the weakest location type for a different reason: name collision, not scarcity. Many African districts share names with their main city or region — Goma and Beni are canonical examples (city, district, regional centre simultaneously). The model defaults to CITY for ambiguous cases because CITY is more often correct. The confusion matrix on slide 20 shows the pattern. Two weak rows, two different causal stories — variability for VICTIM, ambiguity for DISTRICT. Telling them apart is what proves the error analysis was real.

9 · Four pre-loaded interrupts

"Why is VICTIM the worst entity? Isn't that the one that matters most for a violence-monitoring system?"
"Two causes. Low support — five thousand four hundred ninety-two gold spans against forty-seven thousand for ACTOR, nearly nine times less training signal. And extreme phrasing variability — anything from 'civilians' to 'Christian worshippers' to 'the bus driver's family' can be a victim, where DATE has a handful of patterns covering nearly everything. Fewest examples, widest variety — the hardest cell in the schema. And yes, it matters most — which is exactly why the training recipe targets it: the ablation on the next slide shows focal-plus-weights lifted VICTIM by eleven F1 points over plain cross-entropy. Zero-point-eight-one-seven is the lifted number. The remaining gap is structural — it needs real-news corpus expansion or span-level CRF boundary refinement, both named in the thesis as limitation two and future work four."
The question has a barb — "the one that matters most" — and the answer embraces it instead of deflecting: because it matters most, the whole training recipe aims at it. That judo turn converts the weakest number into the motivation for the contribution. End on the forward reference; do not unpack the ablation table here.
"Precision is higher than recall on every single row. Doesn't that mean the model is systematically missing entities?"
"It means the model is calibrated to miss rather than to guess — and that's by design. When uncertain, it defaults to O instead of risking a wrong entity. The cost is some missed entities; the benefit is fewer false alarms. For an analyst reviewing extracted records, that's the right direction: a false alarm costs a deletion and erodes trust in every other chip on the screen; a miss costs an addition the analyst was scanning for anyway. And the trade is adjustable — the per-category confidence thresholds from the inference pipeline can be lowered for a recall-hungry use case. Section 4.7 documents the knob. The direction is a choice, stated openly, not an accident."
Mechanism → direction-is-right-for-consumer → tunable knob. The phrase "calibrated to miss rather than to guess" reframes the observation as design before the panellist can frame it as flaw. The false-alarm-erodes-trust point is the operational heart: precision failures poison the analyst's trust in everything; recall failures cost only the one missed span.
"DISTRICT has four times the training data of VICTIM but almost the same F1. Doesn't that contradict your low-support explanation?"
"No — it shows there are two different failure modes, and support only explains one of them. VICTIM is scarcity plus variability: few examples, unbounded phrasings. DISTRICT is ambiguity: plenty of examples, but many African districts share names with their main city or their region — Goma is simultaneously a city, a district, and a regional centre. For an ambiguous mention, the model defaults to CITY because that's more often correct, and every such default is a DISTRICT miss. More DISTRICT data wouldn't fix that — the surface form is genuinely ambiguous; disambiguation needs context the sentence often doesn't contain. The confusion matrix on slide twenty shows exactly this CITY-DISTRICT confusion pattern. CASUALTIES is the mirror case — rare but formulaic, so it scores well. Support predicts F1 only when the phrasing distribution is held constant."
This is the smartest attack the table invites — it tests whether the causal stories are real or post-hoc. The answer's strength is naming the full two-by-two: VICTIM (rare + variable, weak), CASUALTIES (rare + formulaic, strong), DISTRICT (common + ambiguous, weak), DATE (common + formulaic, strongest). Four cells, four entities, one principle: support × phrasing-regularity jointly predict F1.
"These F1 differences between entities — are they even significant, or just noise on the rare rows?"
"The seed variance is about plus-or-minus zero-point-four points on macro F1, and rare entities do have wider per-row variance — that's acknowledged. But the structure I'm reading from the table is far above that noise floor: the DATE-to-VICTIM spread is fourteen points, the tier boundaries are three-to-five points apart, and the rankings are stable across all three seeds. What I don't lean on is any single one-point difference — REGION versus CASUALTIES at 0.891 versus 0.885 is within noise and the slide draws no conclusion from it. The operational tiers are built on the robust gaps, not the fragile ones."
Concede the per-row variance immediately, then distinguish the robust structure (14-point spread, stable rankings) from the fragile differences (adjacent rows). Naming a specific difference you do NOT defend (REGION vs CASUALTIES) is the honest-metric reframe again — policing your own table harder than the panel would. That one move usually ends the line of questioning.

Why this line is the hero

  • It converts metrics into a usability claim. Eight decimals become five workflow instructions. This is the thesis's operational selling point — the per-field map is what makes review-not-rewrite actually save analyst time — delivered as a single spoken paragraph.
  • Parallel structure does the memorisation for the panel. Five clauses, same shape: field → instruction. The panel will remember "trust dates, correct victims" long after the decimals fade — and that is the right residue for this slide to leave.
  • It pre-owns both weak rows. "Districts and victims need the most correction" states the slide's two worst numbers as workflow fact, in your framing, before any question. Weakest-number ownership in one clause.
  • Consistent with the deck's posture. Slide 16 demoted accuracy and declined relaxed scoring; slide 17 reads its own table's weaknesses out loud and bands them into instructions. Same honesty discipline, now applied row by row: operational truth over flattering summary.

12 · Quiz

Q1. The panel asks: "Why is VICTIM your weakest entity when victims are what a violence-monitoring system is for?"

Pick the strongest response.

  • "VICTIM is inherently the hardest NER class; every published system struggles with it, so 0.817 is in line with expectations."
  • "The 0.817 figure is actually competitive — most fields would consider anything above 0.8 production-ready."
  • "Two causes: low support — 5,492 spans vs 47,612 for ACTOR — and extreme phrasing variability, from 'civilians' to 'the bus driver's family'. And because it matters most, the training recipe targets it: the ablation on the next slide shows +11 F1 over plain cross-entropy. 0.817 is the lifted number; the remaining gap is structural — limitation 2 and future work 4 name the paths."
  • "Victim identification is ultimately subjective, so a ceiling around 0.82 reflects annotator disagreement rather than model weakness."

Q2. Precision exceeds recall on every row. What is the rehearsed three-step reading?

Pick the complete version.

  • It shows the model is under-trained — more epochs would close the precision-recall gap and should be future work.
  • Mechanism: when uncertain the model defaults to O, trading misses for fewer false alarms. Direction: right for analyst review — a false alarm costs a deletion and erodes trust in every chip; a miss costs one addition the analyst was scanning for anyway. Knob: per-category confidence thresholds (§4.7) can trade precision for recall per use case.
  • It is a known artefact of BIO encoding — the B/I split systematically depresses recall, and switching to BIOES would equalise the columns.
  • It reflects the 78 % O dominance: with so many O tokens, precision on entity classes is mechanically inflated relative to recall.

Q3. "DISTRICT has 4× VICTIM's training data but nearly the same F1 — doesn't that break your low-support explanation?"

Pick the strongest response.

  • "District performance is dragged down by inconsistent gold annotations — the κ for DISTRICT was the lowest in the pilot."
  • "They're within noise of each other, so the comparison doesn't carry meaning either way."
  • "DISTRICT spans are longer on average, and strict span matching penalises long spans more, depressing the F1."
  • "Two different failure modes. VICTIM is scarcity plus variability. DISTRICT is ambiguity with plenty of data — districts share names with their cities and regions (Goma: city, district, regional centre simultaneously), and the model defaults to CITY because it's more often right. More data wouldn't fix genuinely ambiguous surface forms. CASUALTIES is the mirror: rare but formulaic, so strong. Support predicts F1 only with phrasing regularity held constant."

Q4. What is the operational-translation move, and why does the slide depend on it?

Pick the correct account.

  • Band the eight F1s into four behavioural tiers (trust / spot-check / verify / correct), phrase each as what the analyst does rather than what the model scores, and connect to the workflow claim: the map is what makes review-not-rewrite save time, because the analyst knows what to skim and what to read carefully. Without it, slide 17 is just more decimals after slide 16.
  • Translate F1 into percentages so non-technical panel members can follow — 0.956 becomes "right 95.6 % of the time".
  • Re-derive each per-entity F1 from its precision and recall on stage, demonstrating command of the underlying arithmetic.
  • Rank entities by operational importance rather than by F1, so the panel sees the system's priorities rather than its scores.

Lesson 06 · Slide 4 — The bottleneck quantified

Turn 30,000 events into 5.5 analysts — and disarm the automation attack

Slide 4 is where the abstract scale on slide 3 becomes concrete labour cost. It's also where you say "VioNER does not replace analysts" — the line that decides how the panel feels about your work for the rest of the talk.

Mission link. Land the operational case AND the no-replacement framing. Skill type. Arithmetic on stage + strategic framing. Time. 15 minutes.

Lesson 5 taught you to deliver the continent-scale numbers in slide 3. Slide 4 does the next move: multiplies the 30k-events figure out into labour cost (~10k analyst-hours ≈ 5.5 full-time analysts), then lands a strategic frame — "VioNER does not replace analysts" — that pre-empts the automation-anxiety attack. Two new skills this lesson teaches: arithmetic delivery and review-not-replace.

2 · The 90-second script, in three beats

1
Beat 1 — per-article time + 7-step workflow ~30 seconds
"The bottleneck, quantified. Each article takes a trained analyst fifteen to twenty-five minutes — read, identify actors, cross-reference against an internal armed-group list, narrow location from country to city, extract casualties with qualifiers, classify the event type, and log."
Seven workflow steps in one sentence. Don't list them like a checklist — chunk them. See Section 3 for the chunking pattern.
2
Beat 2 — the FTE arithmetic ~20 seconds
"At thirty thousand events that's about ten thousand analyst-hours per year — roughly five and a half full-time analysts whose entire job is article-to-row conversion."
Three derived numbers in 20 seconds: 30k events → 10k hours → 5.5 FTE. See Section 4 for arithmetic chaining.
3
Beat 3 — the strategic framing ~40 seconds
"Key framing: VioNER does not replace analysts — it turns an article-READING task into an article-REVIEWING task. Reviewing is lower cognitive load than coding from blank, so per-article time drops. Even halving it frees three analysts to cover under-monitored theatres."
This is the strategic spine of slides 3-7. Land "does not replace" with conviction; emphasise READING vs REVIEWING vocally; end on "theatres" with a clean stop.

3 · The 7-step workflow — chunking pattern

Saying seven workflow steps in one breath sounds like a robot reading a checklist. Group them into four chunks that feel like natural pairs or descriptions. The panel hears four meaningful clusters instead of seven equal items.

Chunk 1 (pair)
read, identify actors
~3 s
Chunk 2 (pair)
cross-reference against an internal armed-group list, narrow location from country to city
~6 s
Chunk 3 (single)
extract casualties with qualifiers
~3 s
Chunk 4 (pair)
classify the event type, and log
~3 s

The "and" before the last item matters

"...classify the event type, and log" — the "and" before "log" signals end-of-list. Without it, the panel doesn't know when to stop counting and starts wondering if there are more steps. This is the same end-of-list rule as the institutional consumers list in slide 3.

4 · The arithmetic chain — delivery technique

You have to say three derived numbers in one breath: 30,000 events → 10,000 hours → 5.5 FTE. The trick is to make the chain feel like one continuous thought, not three separate calculations.

The arithmetic-delivery rules

  1. Don't show your work on stage. The intermediate "600,000 minutes" step lives in the script-writer's head, not in your mouth. You say "at 30k events that's about 10k hours" — the panel does the multiplication silently.
  2. Round, don't precision. Say "ten thousand analyst-hours", not "ten-thousand-two-hundred". Precision creates fragility; round numbers are memorable.
  3. Use the word "roughly". "Roughly five and a half full-time analysts" — concedes approximation, immunises against "exactly how did you arrive at this?" attacks.
  4. Three numbers in 20 seconds means each gets ~6 seconds. That's enough for one clause each, with a half-beat pause between.

5 · The "review not replace" framing — the strategic spine

Beat 3 is the most strategically important thing you say on slides 3-7. It pre-empts the automation-anxiety attack that almost every defence panel raises — explicitly or implicitly.

The hero line — memorise verbatim "VioNER does not replace analysts — it turns an article-READING task into an article-REVIEWING task."
Attack pre-emptedHow this line defuses it
"Are you putting analysts out of work?""Does not replace" — neutralised. You're not automating away the role.
"What's the practical benefit?"READING vs REVIEWING — review is lower cognitive load, so per-article time drops.
"Won't analysts resist using this?"You're making their job easier, not redundant. The framing aligns with worker interest.
"Are you sure this saves time in practice?""Even halving it" — the conservative claim is robust; you're not promising 10x improvement.

Vocal emphasis on READING and REVIEWING

The READING / REVIEWING contrast is the conceptual heart of slide 4. Mark them in your script and deliver each with subtle vocal weight:

  • Slight emphasis on READING — say it like a tired analyst would say it.
  • Slight emphasis on REVIEWING — say it like a relieved analyst would say it.
  • The contrast between the two delivers more meaning than the words alone.

6 · The three pre-loaded attacks

"Where do the 15-25 minutes come from?"
"Consistent with ACLED's published coder-throughput documentation and confirmed during UAT interviews with two early-warning analysts. With practice it can drop to ten minutes for routine articles; complex multi-event articles can take forty-five minutes or more."
The range concedes variability. ACLED is the keyword that grounds the answer.
"Are you trying to replace human analysts?"
"No — the output is explicitly framed as a triage layer. Analysts review and edit before any record is published. Recommendation one in section seven-point-four says to treat output as triage, not final. The role isn't automated away; it's repositioned higher up the cognitive ladder."
"Triage layer" is the operationally precise term. Use it.
"How does VioNER actually save time, concretely?"
"By turning reading-from-blank into reviewing-pre-extracted-output. Conservative estimates from comparable NLP-assisted coding workflows in other domains suggest forty to sixty percent time reduction. We don't claim to have measured that in production — recommendation one in section seven-point-four is the controlled pilot study."
"We don't claim to have measured" — concede what you don't know. That's the honest hedge that protects the rest of the claim.

8 · Quiz

Q1. Why state the FTE arithmetic (5.5 analysts) on this slide rather than just saying "many analyst-hours"?

Pick the strongest reason.

  • To show you can do basic multiplication.
  • Because the FTE number is the headline metric of the thesis.
  • 5.5 FTE makes the cost feel concrete and human-sized. "10,000 hours" is abstract; "five and a half full-time analysts" is staffable, budgetable, real.
  • Because the panel might check the math and you want to be precise.

Q2. A panellist asks: "Aren't you trying to replace human analysts?"

Pick the strongest framing for your one-sentence answer.

  • "The model is just a tool — it's the analysts who decide what to do with it."
  • "No — the output is explicitly framed as a triage layer. Analysts review and edit before any record is published. The role isn't automated away; it's repositioned higher up the cognitive ladder."
  • "VioNER can only handle 91% of cases — humans are still needed for the rest."
  • "The system is designed to augment, not replace, human judgement."

Q3. What does the "even halving it frees three analysts" line do strategically?

Pick the function of this specific claim.

  • Establishes the operational impact under a conservative assumption — so the claim is robust even if VioNER doesn't deliver the full 40-60% reduction.
  • Promises the panel that VioNER will save half the labour cost.
  • Suggests three analysts will be reassigned to other roles.
  • Demonstrates that VioNER's improvements are measurable in person-equivalents.

  • I can say "thirty thousand events → ten thousand analyst-hours → five and a half FTE" smoothly in 20 seconds.
  • I use the word "roughly" before the FTE number to concede approximation.
  • I can land "VioNER does not replace analysts" with conviction, not as a throwaway.
  • I emphasise READING and REVIEWING vocally — the contrast carries the meaning.
  • I have all three pre-loaded answers (15-25 min source, replacement attack, time-saving mechanism) ready to deliver in 10 seconds each.

If all seven are ticked, slide 4 is armoured. You've converted slide 3's continent-scale claim into a concrete labour-cost case AND pre-empted the automation-anxiety attack that will otherwise dog the entire defence. From slide 5 onward, the panel has accepted that the problem is real and that you're solving it the right way.

Pre-loaded interrupts

Q · "Why is BERT 75 % of latency?"
110 M params on CPU — matrix mults dominate. Other steps ~10 % combined. Paste, wait, read cadence ⇒ 150 ms is well inside operational target. CPU was the deployment-target choice (non-ML engineers, standard server).
Q · "What does 'normalised' mean for the date?"
Relative dates anchored to article's publication date. Friday-published "Tuesday" ⇒ prior Tuesday's ISO date. Absolute dates parsed directly. Without ⇒ range-query analytics can't filter by date.
Q · "How is 'at least' preserved?"
Post-processor parses CASUALTIES into {killed: 12, qualifier: 'at least'}. Examples: "as many as 30" ⇒ {30, "as many as"}; "dozens" ⇒ {null, "dozens"}. Hedging preserved ⇒ analytics neither over-states confidence nor drops hedged counts.
Q · "What if KB lookup fails?"
Record still persists with raw surface form. No fabrication. Slide 13's two guarantees: validation never blocks; enrichment never invents. New armed groups land exactly here.

Delivery traps to avoid

  • Stumbling on steps 4–6 (BIO decode / confidence filter / 5W1H group) ⇒ panel reads "doesn't know own pipeline". Drill list eyes-closed × 5.
  • Per-step millisecond math in Beat 2 ⇒ overruns budget. Three numbers + in-memory line is enough.
  • Rushing the three normalisation flourishes ⇒ they are the slide's highest-bandwidth moments. Pause on each.
  • Adding a fourth flourish (e.g. KB enrichment of Al-Shabaab) ⇒ steals slide 13's payoff. Three is the right number.
  • Deploying per-entity F1 numbers ⇒ steals slide 17's payoff. Slide 15 demonstrates capability; slide 17 measures it.
  • Skipping the slide-14 verbatim callback in Beat 4 ⇒ demonstration sits in isolation. The callback is what makes it satisfy the prior claim.

$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{focal}} \;=\; \alpha_y \;\times\; (1 - p_y)^\gamma \;\times\; -\log p_y$$

Cross-entropy term

$-\log p_y$ — the "surprise". Big when the model gave low probability to the true class.

Focal factor

$(1-p_y)^\gamma$ — collapses to ~0 when $p_y$ is near 1. Suppresses easy-correct tokens.

Class weight $\alpha_y$

Per-class multiplier; inverse-frequency with cap at 10. Boosts rare classes across the dataset.

What "production loss" means

All three multiplied together, plus label smoothing β = 0.1 on the target distribution.

The 30-second pitch

"Focal loss is cross-entropy multiplied by a $(1-p_y)^2$ focusing factor that suppresses easy-correct tokens, paired with inverse-frequency class weights so rare classes contribute meaningfully to the gradient. The ablation in §6.6 shows the combination lifted VICTIM F1 by 11 points over plain cross-entropy without hurting any other entity."

Three attacks, three answers
1
Why γ = 2?
Literature default (Lin et al. 2017). Tested γ = 1 (smaller gains) and γ = 3 (training instability). γ = 2 is the empirically stable choice.
2
Why cap at 10?
Uncapped, VICTIM hits α ≈ 22 → early-epoch gradients blow up → optimiser overshoots. Empirically, 10 keeps rare classes elevated without destabilising training.
3
Why focal + class weights, not just one?
They attack different aspects: class weights rebalance across classes; focal loss rebalances within a class. Combination gives +11 on VICTIM; focal alone +8; weights alone +7.

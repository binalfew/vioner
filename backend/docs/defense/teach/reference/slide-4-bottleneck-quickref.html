Beat 1 — per-article time + 7-step workflow (30 s)
Chunk the seven steps into four clusters "The bottleneck, quantified. Each article takes a trained analyst fifteen to twenty-five minutes — read, identify actors · cross-reference against an internal armed-group list, narrow location from country to city · extract casualties with qualifiers · classify the event type, and log."
Beat 2 — the FTE arithmetic (20 s)
Three numbers in 20 s. Use "roughly". "At thirty thousand events that's about ten thousand analyst-hours per year — roughly five and a half full-time analysts whose entire job is article-to-row conversion."
Beat 3 — strategic framing (40 s)
Emphasise READING vs REVIEWING. End on "theatres" cleanly. "Key framing: VioNER does not replace analysts — it turns an article-READING task into an article-REVIEWING task. Reviewing is lower cognitive load than coding from blank, so per-article time drops. Even halving it frees three analysts to cover under-monitored theatres."
The hero line — memorise verbatim "VioNER does not replace analysts — it turns an article-READING task into an article-REVIEWING task."

Three pre-loaded attacks
1
Where do the 15-25 minutes come from?
ACLED's published coder-throughput + UAT interviews with 2 early-warning analysts. 10 min for routine, 45+ for complex multi-event.
2
Are you trying to replace human analysts?
No — output is a triage layer. Analysts review and edit before publication. §7.4 recommendation 1: treat output as triage, not final. Role repositioned higher up the cognitive ladder.
3
How does VioNER actually save time?
By turning reading-from-blank into reviewing-pre-extracted-output. Conservative estimate 40-60% reduction. Don't claim to have measured in production — §7.4 recommendation 1 is the controlled pilot.
Arithmetic-delivery rules
1. Don't show your work on stage. Intermediate steps stay in the script-writer's head.
2. Round, don't precision. "10k hours" not "10,240 hours".
3. Use "roughly" before FTE — concedes approximation, immunises against precision attacks.
4. Three numbers in 20 sec = ~6 sec each.
